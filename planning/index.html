01 / Decision brief

Build the story around one damaged shipment becoming a reviewable case. FreightClaimKit should feel like a precise, considered enterprise company: physical cargo, transparent evidence, explicit responsibility and human judgment. Its first commercial experiment remains an evidence-completeness audit. All five offers remain distinct and visible.

The recommended narrative is incident → evidence → discrepancy → authority → reviewer action → evaluation. A continuous cargo-and-dossier object gives the site coherence; different chapter compositions give it variety. Luxury comes from typography, material quality, measured pacing and exact information, rather than constant spectacle.

DecisionSpecification
CompanyFreightClaimKit. Concept 42; logistics and knowledge sector. Freight Trestle is the superseded working name from the supplied brief.
Buyer / userU.S. shipper or 3PL claims manager with recurring damage/shortage cases and an accountable budget; claims analyst operates the workbench. Contracting/insurance counsel validates standing and permitted handling.
PositioningA provenance-first evidence audit before anyone files a freight claim. Proposed value: make evidence gaps, contradictory values and review ownership visible.

Claim statusResearch-stage concept. No real customers, integrations, revenue, recovery improvement, operating SaaS or accepted pricing has been demonstrated.
First wedgeFreight Desk: a standalone, permissioned retrospective 20-case evidence audit for one carrier/mode, with named rule and workflow owners.
Instruction handling. The current request controls: use FreightClaimKit and give the uploaded master guide priority over conflicting brief wording. Historical naming/domain notes and internal build decisions are preserved in the handoff rather than presented as current company claims. Public research/methodology content can explain the validation plan without exposing an internal instruction dump.

StepOwner taskProtocol
01Identify the movementRecord carrier legal entity, carrier/forwarder/insurer role, mode, service, origin/destination and relevant legs. Do not infer jurisdiction from a brand name.
